# Scientific Writing Principles

## Overview

Effective scientific writing is clear, concise, accurate, and objective. It aims to communicate complex information to a specific audience as efficiently as possible.

## 1. Clarity

**Precision**: Use the most specific word possible. Avoid vague terms like "thing," "stuff," "deal with," or "look at."
* **Weak**: We looked at the cells.
* **Strong**: We examined the morphological changes in the hepatocytes.

**Definition**: Define technical terms and all abbreviations at their first mention in the text.
* Example: "We used quantitative polymerase chain reaction (qPCR) to..."

**Logical Flow**: Ensure each sentence follows logically from the previous one, and each paragraph from the one before it. Use transitional phrases (e.g., "Furthermore," "In contrast," "Consequently") to signal relationships between ideas.

**Simplicity**: Avoid unnecessary jargon. If a simple word works as well as a complex one, use the simple one.

## 2. Conciseness

**Eliminate Redundancy**: Remove words that don't add meaning.
* **Wordy**: "At this point in time" (4 words)
* **Concise**: "Now" (1 word)
* **Wordy**: "Due to the fact that" (4 words)
* **Concise**: "Because" (1 word)

**Favor Active Voice**: While passive voice has its place in Methods, active voice is usually clearer and shorter.
* **Passive**: The samples were analyzed by the team. (6 words)
* **Active**: The team analyzed the samples. (5 words)

**Strong Verbs**: Avoid turning verbs into nouns (nominalization).
* **Weak**: We performed an analysis of the data.
* **Strong**: We analyzed the data.

## 3. Accuracy

**Exact Values**: Report exact p-values and measurements rather than just "significant" or "large."

**Consistent Terminology**: Use the same term for the same concept throughout the manuscript. Do not use synonyms just for variety, as this can confuse readers about whether you're referring to the same thing.

**Hedge Appropriately**: Use qualifiers like "suggests," "indicates," or "may" when conclusions are not absolute. Avoid overstating your findings.

## 4. Objectivity

**Neutral Tone**: Avoid emotional or superlative language (e.g., "amazing," "incredible," "terrible").

**Focus on Data**: Let the results speak for themselves. Avoid using the first person ("I" or "We") excessively if it shifts focus from the research to the researcher.

**Acknowledge Counter-Evidence**: Fairly represent and discuss evidence that contradicts your hypotheses or findings.

## 5. Paragraph Structure

**One Idea per Paragraph**: Each paragraph should focus on a single, coherent concept.

**Topic Sentences**: Start each paragraph with a sentence that summarizes the main point of that paragraph.

**Supporting Sentences**: Follow the topic sentence with evidence, data, or explanation that supports the main point.

**Concluding/Transition Sentences**: End the paragraph by summarizing the idea or linking it to the next paragraph.

---

## Writing Checklist

- [ ] Is every technical term defined at first use?
- [ ] Are all abbreviations defined at first use?
- [ ] Can any words or sentences be removed without losing meaning?
- [ ] Does every paragraph have a clear topic sentence?
- [ ] Is the tone professional and neutral?
- [ ] Are the findings supported by the data presented?
- [ ] Have all reporting guidelines been followed?
